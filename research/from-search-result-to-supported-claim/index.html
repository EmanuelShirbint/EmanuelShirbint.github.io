Evidence practice · public research records

From search result to supported claim

A five-field test for turning public discovery into a sentence the underlying record can actually support.

Status: Primary-source evidence guide · Current public-record snapshot · No new empirical result · No citation-count claim · No employment claim · No advisory claim · No clinical-authority claim

Discovery is the beginning, not the evidence

A public name search can locate a profile, article, preprint, conference record, catalogue entry, or third-party aggregation. It cannot by itself prove that two records identify the same person, that a work was peer-reviewed, that a deposited affiliation remains current, or that a platform metric is comparable across services.

The usable unit is therefore not the search result. It is a bounded claim joined to a primary record and its status metadata.

A reusable evidence-safe sentence pattern

[Primary source] identifies [person] as an author of [exact work and version], classified as [status], and supports only [bounded claim]. It does not establish [explicitly excluded inference].

This pattern keeps identity, work lineage, version, publication status, and claim scope visible in one sentence. It also makes later correction possible: a new publisher version can update the version field without rewriting a conference record as a journal article, a preprint as peer-reviewed work, or a publication affiliation as current employment.

Current registry interpretation

On 1 September 2026, a targeted Crossref created-date query returned no new exact-family record since the prior run; Europe PMC remained at 14 author-query records and PubMed at four exact-author records. Both checked Preprints.org pages still marked Version 2 as latest and not peer-reviewed. No new canonical work or version was promoted into the catalogue.

Citation metadata was inspected only as time-sensitive registry metadata and remains excluded from public claims because database coverage, version deduplication, and counting rules are not normalized into the canonical claim ledger.

Claim boundaries across the public profile

Peer-reviewed work, conference work, preprints, conceptual research, the bounded empirical preprint, the locally evidenced EWCC production system, employment, and advisory work remain separate evidence classes. This guide reports no new scientific finding and makes no claim of production validation, clinical use, treatment authority, regulatory status, present employment, advisory engagement, institutional representation, or citation impact.
