The record type controls the sentence

A search for TotalSpineSeg can surface a public preprint, an ISMRM conference record, an open-source repository, package releases, and third-party references. These records are related, but they answer different questions. A preprint reports a study, a conference identifier records a conference contribution, and a source repository exposes an implementation. None silently inherits the status of another.

The evidence-safe approach is to identify the research lineage once, attach each claim to the record that supports it, and preserve the status of every manifestation.

Why this remains one catalogue lineage

Titles can differ across a manuscript, conference abstract, and software documentation while still referring to the same underlying project. Deduplication therefore uses more than title similarity: author overlap, topic, timing, persistent identifiers, citation instructions, and explicit cross-links are considered together. In the canonical catalogue, the TotalSpineSeg preprint, ISMRM contribution, and implementation remain connected manifestations rather than three independently counted research works.

This does not erase the conference record or reduce the software to a citation. It preserves both while preventing double counting. The conference work remains conference work; the preprint remains a preprint; the repository remains software evidence.

A claim ladder for mixed research outputs

  1. Existence: the linked record exists and exposes the stated title or implementation.
  2. Authorship or contribution: the specific record lists Emanuel Shirbint; attribution stays work-specific.
  3. Reported result: numerical or empirical statements are attributed to the preprint, not restated as independently verified performance.
  4. Dissemination: the conference DOI supports conference presentation status only.
  5. Availability: the repository supports public software availability, not clinical readiness.
  6. Higher-status claims: journal peer review, production validation, clinical use, regulatory status, present employment, or advisory authority require separate direct evidence.
