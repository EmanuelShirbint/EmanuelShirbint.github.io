Evidence guide · governed self-improvement

Seven cycles, bounded claims

What a governed recursive self-improvement experiment supports—and what its public record explicitly does not establish.

Status: Version 2 preprint · Empirical research · Bounded local scaffolding-level experiment · Not peer-reviewed · No production-validation claim · No citation-count claim

The evidence in one sentence

The checked Version 2 publisher record reports seven bounded cycles from a cryptographically frozen baseline: four promoted successor states, one failed non-promoted transition retained in the history, and two consecutive NO_CHANGE outcomes, with every promotion requiring separate human authorization.

Why the outcome sequence matters

Observed outcomeEvidence-backed interpretationBoundary
Four promoted statesSeveral operational governance capabilities passed the experiment's bounded evaluation and human-promotion path.This does not establish open-ended, exponential, or general capability growth.
One failed transitionThe failed third cycle remained a failed historical event; a later boundary revision was prospective rather than a rewrite of the result.Preserving a failure is evidence about governance behaviour, not proof that every future failure will be detected.
Two NO_CHANGE outcomesCycles 6 and 7 created no successor when the bounded search found no qualifying deficiency.NO_CHANGE is a negative result under the tested protocol, not proof of permanent saturation.

What was—and was not—self-improving

Established by the public record

Scaffolding-level change

The study classifies the experiment as scaffolding-level self-modification: validators, lineage controls, evidence handling, and related operational artifacts changed while the foundation model remained fixed.

Not established

Unbounded autonomous growth

The record does not demonstrate artificial general intelligence, self-training of model weights, unbounded or exponential recursive improvement, autonomous promotion, or permanent saturation.

The authority boundary

Candidate changes could not directly modify their parent states, the governance-sensitive layer, authoritative external systems, or historical evidence. The machine path could recommend a candidate, but a separate human acceptance authority controlled promotion. This supports a claim about the tested governance architecture; it does not convert the experiment into evidence of unrestricted autonomy.
