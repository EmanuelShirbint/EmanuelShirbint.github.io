Evidence guide · LLM-agent safety

Beyond pass rates: what survives after the guardrail?

A source-led guide to two proposed structural tests for distinguishing measured improvement from hidden persistence in LLM-agent safety evaluation.

Status: Version 1 preprints · Not peer-reviewed · Empirical studies · Synthetic pilots · No production-agent validation claimed

The evaluation problem

A lower attack-success or policy-violation rate is useful evidence, but it does not by itself show why the rate fell. A safety intervention may have removed a reusable failure route, or it may have suppressed the measured examples while a related route remains available under different pressure. The two public preprints treat frequency metrics as necessary but incomplete and ask a structural follow-up question: which safe and unsafe behavioural patterns persist as adversarial pressure increases?

Two linked proposals

Preprint · Synthetic pilot

Topological robustness and anti-fragility

The first framework represents responses, decisions, and tool-call trajectories as a pressure-indexed structure. It proposes testing whether a safe core remains stable and whether controlled hardening improves the before/after structure on held-out attacks.

The reported pilot used 1,440 generated traces. Its stated scope is a controlled proof of concept with known ground truth, not external validation over production LLMs.

Preprint · Synthetic pilot

Pressure-filtered persistence and the Masking Index

The follow-up study proposes a diagnostic for the specific case in which development-suite frequency improves while held-out unsafe structure remains. The Masking Index is intended to separate apparent cleaning from structural hardening under stated correspondence and stability assumptions.

The reported instrument-validation study used 5,760 generated traces with 32 repetitions per cell. The paper says this validates the detector on planted synthetic ground truth, not predictive value on real agents.

How to read the evidence

QuestionSupported interpretationUnsupported leap
Did measured failures become less frequent?Use held-out frequency estimates and uncertainty intervals.Assuming a lower rate proves the underlying route disappeared.
Did unsafe structure persist under pressure?Use the proposed pressure-filtered summaries as a complementary diagnostic.Treating a synthetic instrument result as production effectiveness.
Did hardening generalize?Require held-out attacks, fixed correspondence rules, and prospective falsification conditions.Reusing the development suite as proof of generalization.
Is the method operationally validated?The checked records establish proposed methods and synthetic pilots.Claiming external, production, regulatory, or peer-reviewed validation.
