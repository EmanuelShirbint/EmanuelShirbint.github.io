A count is inseparable from its rule

Public research systems index different record types and apply different matching rules. One may include only biomedical journal records, another may retain multiple DOI versions, and a curated library may merge revised titles and related manifestations into one research lineage. The numbers are therefore comparable only after the counted unit is named.

Where the extra records come from

The Reinforcement Learning Contour and Bounded Recursive Self-Improvement each have Version 1 and Version 2 DOI records. The canonical library preserves the earlier DOI as provenance but counts each title lineage once. The Human Digital Twins record also has an earlier public title/DOI lineage that is retained by registries while the curated library uses the revised public record. These are record-level differences, not evidence of additional peer-reviewed works.

Conference records require another distinction. The 2024 spine-segmentation conference DOI and the 2025 TotalSpineSeg conference DOI remain conference work. The TotalSpineSeg preprint, conference contribution, repository, and package distribution are connected as manifestations of a research lineage; software availability does not create a new empirical result or journal publication.

A safe way to report any publication number

  1. Name the source: PubMed, Europe PMC, Crossref, or the canonical library.
  2. Name the unit: registry records, DOI versions, conference contributions, or deduplicated works.
  3. Name the status: peer-reviewed journal article, conference work, preprint, conceptual research, or bounded empirical preprint.
  4. State the date: registry snapshots change as records propagate.
  5. Keep impact separate: a publication-record count is not a citation count, quality score, employment fact, or advisory credential.
