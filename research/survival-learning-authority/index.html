Survival, learning, authority

Three questions that keep safety evidence, epistemic change, and permission to act from collapsing into one unsupported claim.

Status: Cross-record evidence synthesis · Source statuses preserved · No new empirical result · No production-validation claim · No clinical-authority claim · No citation-count claim

Why three questions are necessary

A system can pass a safety test without showing that unsafe structure disappeared. It can revise its working mechanism without earning authority to promote its own changes. It can produce a well-supported explanation without gaining permission to intervene. The checked public records treat these as three separate evidence problems:

  1. Survival: what safe or unsafe structure persists when pressure changes?
  2. Learning: what changed in the mechanism that will shape the next inquiry?
  3. Authority: who is permitted to promote a change or authorize an action?

The non-substitution rule

Safety evidence

A lower failure rate is not structural proof

Frequency evidence and persistence evidence answer different questions. Neither may be silently substituted for the other.

Learning evidence

A changed mechanism is not self-authorization

Evidence of bounded scaffolding change does not transfer promotion authority from a separate human acceptance role to the changing system.

Action evidence

Confidence is not permission

An explanation or recommendation can justify review without authorizing intervention, especially in a clinical context.

A practical evidence ladder

  1. State the exact record and version being used.
  2. Preserve its status: conceptual preprint, empirical synthetic pilot, or bounded local experiment.
  3. Ask which of the three questions the evidence actually answers.
  4. Require separate evidence for any step from observation to mechanism change, and from mechanism change to authorized action.
  5. Keep falsifying outcomes—including failures and NO_CHANGE—inside the public interpretation rather than reporting promotions alone.
