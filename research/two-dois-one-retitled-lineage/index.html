The important distinction: a DOI identifies a record; it does not automatically define the scholarly unit a curated catalogue should count. Conversely, a catalogue merge must not erase either DOI, title, date, or source record. Both identifiers remain visible as provenance.

Why “Version 1” is not enough

The suffix v1 is scoped to each DOI root. Because these records have different DOI roots, their shared Version 1 labels do not establish an ordered Version 1 → Version 2 relationship. Chronology comes from the publisher dates; lineage comes from reconciliation of authors, subject, title continuity, record content, and the maintained source manifest. The catalogue therefore labels the merge as a curatorial conclusion and preserves the primary records so it can be audited or revised.

A safe citation and counting rule

  1. Cite the exact DOI and title actually read.
  2. Label either record as a preprint and explicitly state that it is not peer-reviewed.
  3. When presenting the curated corpus, count the pair as one research lineage and disclose the merge rule.
  4. When reporting registry coverage, count the two registry records separately because that is what the registries expose.
  5. Do not convert record counts or registry citation fields into an impact claim without a normalized, time-stamped citation ledger.

Claim boundaries

This guide reconciles two conceptual-preprint records only. It does not change or substantiate peer-reviewed journal work, conference work, empirical preprints, software, production systems, employment history, advisory work, clinical use, diagnostic authority, or citation impact. Those remain separate evidence classes with separate sources.
